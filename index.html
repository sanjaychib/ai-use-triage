AI literacy · EU AI Act

How much care does this AI use need?

Teams are adopting AI tools faster than policies can follow. Sort six real workplace uses into the risk levels set by the EU AI Act, and see what each level means in practice.

Why it matters now. Since February 2025 the AI Act requires organisations that provide or deploy AI systems to ensure their staff have sufficient AI literacy (Article 4). It also reaches Swiss companies whose AI systems or outputs are used in the EU. Summary for learning purposes, not legal advice.
1 · Four levels

The risk levels in brief

ProhibitedPractices that are banned outright, such as emotion recognition at work or social scoring.
High-riskAllowed, but with strict duties: risk management, data quality, human oversight, records. Includes listed uses in employment and education.
Transparency dutyPeople must be told: when they talk to a chatbot, or when realistic content is AI-generated.
Minimal riskMost everyday uses. No specific AI Act duties, but good practice and internal policy still apply.

4 · Take it to work

AI use triage card

Four questions before a team starts using an AI tool

  1. Does it read people’s emotions at work or in education, or score people socially? Stop: likely prohibited.
  2. Does it help decide on hiring, promotion, task allocation, performance, admission or grading? Treat as high-risk: involve legal and compliance, document the system and keep a human in the decision.
  3. Do people interact with it, or does it create realistic images, audio or video of people? Disclose it clearly.
  4. None of the above? Likely minimal risk. Still check data protection, confidentiality and accuracy, and train the people using it.

Based on Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act), Articles 4, 5 and 50 and Annex III. Simplified for training use, not legal advice.
